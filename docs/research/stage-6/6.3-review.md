## What these rounds establish, and what they do not

Nine rounds is not nine independent opinions on the finished slice: eight reviewed
a **checkpoint**, and only the pending ninth looks at the assembled candidate. The
per-checkpoint reviews were run against exact SHAs, none of the reviewers
modified the candidate, and every P0 and P1 they returned is fixed in the tree at
`f4196fb`.

The recurring finding class across all four checkpoint reviews was **a claim out
running the code that does not match the code** — a rank parsed more loosely than
its own field names, two fields standing in for one, an unchecked leak between
the main screen and its detail screen, an error swallowed into a
self-contradictory snapshot, a build break that made a whole checkpoint's claims
unverifiable. None was a wrong measurement of the product surface; every one was a
gap between a written claim and the implementation beside it, which is the same
class Stage 6.1's fourteen rounds documented and the reason the mechanical parity
register exists at all.
