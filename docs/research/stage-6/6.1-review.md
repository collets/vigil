## What the review must cover

Stage 6.1 is a measurement and planning slice, so the reviewer's job is not to
find a bug in code that does not exist. It is to attack the **measurements, the
classifications, the requirement amendments and the plans**. Specifically:

1. **Re-derive the counts independently.** Build the binary, walk the Cobra
   command tree, and confirm 62 `project` commands, 3 `resources`, 6 root leaf
   commands and 14 `apply` envelope kinds — 85 register entries. Then re-derive
   the 64 `E` / 15 `C` / 6 `X` classification, the 4 fully expressed / 9 partial
   / 72 unreached figures, and the 3 / 5 / 12 decision-class figures. Any
   discrepancy is a finding; a small one is still a finding, because the
   documents present these as exact.
2. **Re-measure the interface claims independently.** Drive
   `vigil dashboard` in a pseudo-terminal against a fresh disposable fixture and
   check: the five views and what each renders; the twenty action strings and
   their command bindings; the `ActiveRun` and `Queue` fields being read but
   never rendered; `InboxEntry.CreatedAt` and `.Blocking` being selected and
   never rendered; the hardcoded `authorize_criteria_changes: false`, the
   hardcoded rationales, the hardcoded `accept` and `pass`, the hardcoded
   `once` scope, and `quality.Target{Kind:"task"}`; the footer truncation at 60
   and 40 columns; and defect 6.1-F17 — that pressing `p` on the History view
   persists a project pause.
3. **Attack the requirement amendments.** R11, R45, R46, R47 and R70 were
   rewritten and P13–P18 were added. Check that each amendment **narrows** to
   something testable and does not lower what the product must do, and that
   none of the ten changes is a quiet scope reduction in the user's favour.
   Check in particular that R46 and R47's "explicitly unavailable" clauses
   resolve the missing cost/usage and implementation-summary capabilities
   honestly rather than deleting the requirement.
4. **Attack the classification rule.** Find a capability the register omits, a
   row classified `E` that the interface genuinely cannot perform with the same
   information, a row classified `C` whose compromise is not the one actually
   forced, or an `X` whose reason is really a scope decision presented as a
   technical impossibility (or the reverse). Conflating the two exclusion
   reasons is a finding.
5. **Attack the sub-stage plans.** For each of 6.2–6.9, look for a plan that
   would break an accepted Stage 5 safeguard, that omits a capability its
   outcome claims, whose completion criteria cannot actually be checked, or
   whose ordering is wrong for a dependency reason. In particular, verify that
   6.2's parity test really walks the real command tree, and that the eight
   sub-stages between them cover all 85 entries and all 20 decision classes
   with no row unowned.
6. **Attack the evidence claims.** The measurements are Linux-only and the
   native macOS attempt is recorded as blocked on authentication. Check that
   no document claims macOS coverage, and that the blockage is reported rather
   than worked around.
7. **Check the repository obligations.** `make docs-check` and `make check`
   pass; every new document is in the index and carries the right tier marker;
   no orphan exists; the status prose is honest about what is and is not
   complete; and the documentation was not quietly softened to make the
   analysis look tidier.
