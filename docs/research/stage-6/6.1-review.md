### The severity tally failed four times, and the reason is structural

Three failures were in the round-1 tally (items 1–3 below); the fourth was the
round-3 verdict table, raised separately as R2F1's successor in the third
round and closed by removing the hand-written totals entirely.

The R-findings tally is derived from the single findings table beneath it, and that
structure is itself the fix for the R-round. It was not there originally, and the
tally was wrong three times because of it:

1. The first review's own summary line mis-tallied its findings as
   "3×P0, 2×P1, 7×P2, 3×P3" when its table held 3/3/5/3. That is the R1 failure
   mode — a headline contradicting the table directly beneath it — reproduced by
   the record written to answer it, and the wrong figure was copied into two
   other documents.
2. The remediation "corrected" it to 3 P0 / 3 P1 / 5 P2 / 4 P3, which was **still
   wrong**: R15 is a P0 and sits in a *second* table, so the original 3/3/5/3 was
   right and the correction invented a P3. The error propagated identically into
   all three places, and one of them — `6.1-parity-gap-analysis.md` — was never
   edited at all and still carried the review's original 3/2/7/3. The third
   review caught it.
3. R15 has now been moved into the single table, so the tally is countable from
   one place, and the correct figures are 4 P0, 3 P1, 5 P2, 3 P3 = 15.

**The generalisable lesson, which is the same one the second review drew about
stale claims:** a figure stated in prose *and* implied by a table is two sources
of truth, and correcting the prose does not correct the table. The only durable
repair is to make the prose *derivable* — one table, counted, no separate
number. This is recorded here rather than quietly corrected because the
recurrence is the finding.

### The first derivation was also wrong, in the same commit

I1's own remediation is the sixth instance of the pattern it describes, and it is
recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because a record that hides its own
recurrence teaches the wrong lesson twice.

**This account was itself wrong when first written, and the sixth review caught
it.** The first version claimed a literal phrase search returned six documents
and that `AGENTS.md` was the missed seventh. Re-run at the baseline `042b835`,
the three methods disagree, and none of them returns six for the reason stated:

| Search method | Finds | What it misses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| literal, space-separated `"three further reviews"` | **5** | `AGENTS.md` (phrase split by a line break) and `stage-5/README.md` (paraphrase) |
| whitespace-flexible, spanning newlines — `perl -0777 -ne 'print "$.\n" if /three\s+further\s+reviews/'` | **6** | `stage-5/README.md` — it shares **no wording** with the others |
| by **claim substance** | **7** | nothing |

The seventh is `docs/plans/stage-5/README.md`, which asserted the identical claim
as a paraphrase — "returned conditional by a narrow follow-up" — and is findable
only by searching for what the documents *say*, not how they spell it. The sixth
is `AGENTS.md`, the one document in the repository that *names the stage-status
trap as its most common failure*, carrying the phrase as `of three further` at the
end of one line and `reviews,` at the start of the next.

So there were **two** distinct blind spots, not one, and the original account
credited the literal search with a hit it could not have produced. The error is
worth keeping precisely because the section's whole purpose is to be an honest
derivation: **the exemplar of "a derivation is only as good as its search"
reported search results its search did not produce.** A count is only a finding
until someone re-runs it.

Two things follow, and both are now in `next-steps.md`:

- **Match strength is ordered, and the order is strict.** A literal phrase is
  weakest, because a document may reword the claim or wrap across a line. A
  whitespace-flexible pattern fixes only the wrapping. Neither can find a
  paraphrase. A set is derived only by **claim substance** — the underlying
  identifiers and the substance of the assertion — and a claim of completeness
  must state which of the three it used.
- **"Clean" is a claim about the search, not about the tree.** Reporting a sweep
  that found nothing, when the sweep could not have found the thing, is worse than
  not reporting it, because it manufactures false assurance in a repository whose
  entire accepted state is a set of such assurances.

This is the same shape as R2F5, where a severity tally was propagated into all
three places while a fourth copy was left untouched. The tally was wrong four
times; the enumeration was wrong once, for the mirror-image reason — it
under-counted rather than over-counted, because the tool's blind spot rather than
the author's memory was the limit. Both are the failure of trusting a method's
output over the thing being measured.

### The mechanism that finally addresses the pattern

Seven rounds of "fix the string, not the claim" produced a mechanical
countermeasure, and it is the reason this round is recorded at length.

**Every countable claim in this record is now re-derived by a script that reads
the documents, and the script fails when a claim does not reproduce.** A claim of
the form "N documents say X", or "this enumeration sums to N", or "the sweep
returns N", is not evidence — it is a hypothesis about a number, and eight rounds
have shown that a competent author asserting one from memory gets it wrong about
as often as not. The checks are:

1. every document asserting 6.1's review state, swept at **claim-substance**
   strength and whitespace-flexibly, agrees on *eight further reviews* and *all
   nine rounds of findings*;
2. the reviewer-method sentence **partitions all nine rounds exactly once, with
   no overlap**, and the stated `1 + 4 + 4 = 9` matches the enumeration;
3. `6.1-R1`…`R15` are **contiguous in one table**, and the recomputed severity
   tally equals the stated **4 P0 / 3 P1 / 5 P2 / 3 P3 = 15**;
4. the verdict table carries **no restated severity totals**;
5. `R01`…`R71` are contiguous with no interrupting prose;
6. the two closed-exclusion-list copies are **byte-identical** (compared by hash,
   not by eye);
7. the next-action floor **equals the previous round's reviewed head** and is a
   real ancestor of the branch tip;
8. **no control character** in any `.md` file;
9. every bare `F<n>` lies inside a `6.1-J*`/`K*`/`L*` cell quoting an erroneous
   reference, and there are none in prose, tables, plans or requirements;
10. `docs/STATUS` still reads `5.7`/`true`/`522cb96`, its `status_documents` list
    is **non-empty**, and all 15 of them carry exactly one matching marker;
11. the slice changed **only** `docs/`, `AGENTS.md` and `README.md`, and the diff
    is required to **resolve and be non-empty** — an unresolvable or empty file
    list is indistinguishable from "clean", which is how check 11 passed
    vacuously until round 9;
12. two **generic** invariants, added because checks scoped to one location
    cannot see a cell that restates a corrected figure: every partition whose
    stated total equals the number of reviews must actually sum to it (a quoted
    stale figure is excluded by construction, not by an allow-list of cells), and
    the Identifier note's count must equal the length of its own list;

    5.7.

and 12 and 13 were absent entirely. **Every check in this list was mutation-tested

check fails** — which is the only thing that distinguishes a check from a
comment. The mutation exercise itself had a fault worth recording: one mutation

verifies its own input is non-empty**, because a check that silently matches

see a line break, and the same failure with opposite polarity. Second, the

about the tree and is subject to exactly the failure it is meant to catch, which
is why its own failures were read rather than suppressed.

falsify it, and falsifying a verified claim to ship a verification tool is a bad
